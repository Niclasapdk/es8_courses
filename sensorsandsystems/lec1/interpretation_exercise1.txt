Sensors & Systems – Interpretation Notes (G1, G2, G3)

Models:
- G1(s) = (0.5s+1)/(s^3 - 3s^2 + 0.4s + 2)
- G2(s) = [ (0.2s^2+2s+1)/(s^2+4s+1) ;
            (0.2s^2+1)/(s^2+4s+1) ]
- G3: xdot = A x + B u, y = C x, with
      A=[-4 -1; 1 0], B=[2;0], C=[0.6 0.4; -0.4 0.4], D=[0;0]

Expected properties:
- G1: observable, unstable
- G2: observable, stable
- G3: observable, stable

------------------------------------------------------------
STEP RESPONSE INTERPRETATION
------------------------------------------------------------

G1 (step):
- Response grows very large with time (diverges).
- Interpretation: unstable dynamics (RHP poles dominate).
- Practical meaning: open-loop unsafe; small excitation can lead to very large output.

G2 (step, 2 outputs):
- Both outputs settle to ~1.
- Output 1: mostly monotonic rise.
- Output 2: initial undershoot then rise.
- Interpretation: stable system with different output-channel transient shapes.

G3 (step, 2 outputs):
- Both outputs settle to ~0.8.
- Output 2 shows initial negative dip before recovery.
- Interpretation: stable state-space system; transient sign change is from output mixing (C matrix), not instability.

------------------------------------------------------------
LSIM INTERPRETATION (bounded realistic input)
Input used:
u(t) = sin(0.8 t) + 0.5*1(t>=5)
------------------------------------------------------------

G1 (lsim):
- Output becomes unbounded (~10^20 scale).
- Key conclusion: bounded input gives unbounded output => unstable (fails BIBO stability).

G2 (lsim):
- Both outputs remain bounded.
- Sinusoid is attenuated and phase-lagged (filtering effect).
- Small transient/kink around t=5 due to step offset in input.
- Channels respond differently because numerators (output paths) differ.

G3 (lsim):
- Bounded outputs, similar lag/attenuation behavior to G2 (same pole dynamics).
- Output 2 can go negative during transient due to C matrix weighting of states.
- Stable and physically consistent multi-output behavior.

------------------------------------------------------------
What to learn from this exercise
------------------------------------------------------------
1) Same system concepts across TF and state-space representations.
2) Stability is visible in plots and confirmed by poles/eigenvalues.
3) Observability answers whether internal states can be inferred from outputs.
4) Multi-output systems can be stable while channels look very different.
5) lsim is crucial: real input tests reveal behavior beyond ideal step tests.
