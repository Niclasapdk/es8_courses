Exercise 2 – Discretization (Ts = 0.5 s), Forward Euler vs Tustin

Continuous models:
G1 unstable
G2 stable
G3 stable

Key formulas:
- Forward Euler pole map: z_FE = 1 + p*Ts
- Tustin pole map:       z_TU = (1 + p*Ts/2)/(1 - p*Ts/2)

Main reason FE looks oscillatory here:
For G2/G3 poles p ≈ -0.268 and -3.732:
- z_FE(slow) = 0.866  (ok)
- z_FE(fast) = -0.866 (negative => alternating sample-to-sample behavior)
This creates the visible staircase overshoot/undershoot.

Tustin gives smoother behavior and closer match to continuous-time for Ts=0.5 s.

------------------------------------------------------------
Interpretation of generated plots
------------------------------------------------------------

1) G1 step: continuous vs FE vs Tustin
- All responses diverge (unstable model).
- Tustin diverges fastest in this simulation.
- FE also unstable/discrete divergent, but appears smaller than Tustin on the same axis scale.

2) G2 step (2 outputs)
- Continuous response is smooth and converges to ~1.
- FE shows zig-zag/alternating overshoot and undershoot around continuous curve.
- Tustin closely follows continuous response.

3) G3 step (2 outputs)
- Same trend as G2 (same pole dynamics).
- FE has staircase oscillatory approximation.
- Tustin closely matches continuous shape and steady-state.

4) G1 lsim: continuous vs FE vs Tustin
- Bounded input produces unbounded output (unstable).
- All methods diverge; Tustin grows fastest in this setup.

5) G2 lsim (2 outputs)
- Continuous outputs remain bounded and smooth.
- FE introduces extra ripple and phase/amplitude distortion.
- Tustin tracks continuous response better.

6) G3 lsim (2 outputs)
- Same conclusion as G2 lsim.
- FE distortion is visible; Tustin is closer to continuous.

------------------------------------------------------------
Conclusions
------------------------------------------------------------

- G1 remains unstable after discretization (both FE and Tustin).
- G2 and G3 remain stable, but FE quality is poor at Ts=0.5 s due to negative mapped fast pole.
- Tustin is the preferred method here for matching continuous dynamics.

Practical takeaway:
If Forward Euler looks too oscillatory, reduce Ts significantly or use Tustin/ZOH.
